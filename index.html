how we work

Four phases, one fixed launch date. You always know what is being built this week and what it costs.

01

Discovery sprint

A two-day workshop with your stakeholders: goals, constraints, systems, data and the risk list. You leave with a scope, a budget range and a date.

02

Architecture & plan

Reference architecture, data model, environments and CI/CD agreed in writing. No development starts before the plan is signed off.

03

Build in two-week sprints

Working software lands in your environment every sprint, with automated tests, a demo and a written status note — no slideware.

04

Launch & support

Cutover runbook, monitoring, alerts and a support rota. We hand over the documentation and stay on call for as long as you need.

faq

How quickly can BuildUrgent start?

Discovery can usually start within five business days, and a full build squad is typically on your project within two to three weeks. Urgent rescues — an unreleased product, a broken migration, an audit deadline — are scheduled as soon as a squad frees up.

Who actually does the work?

A named squad of senior engineers, a product/UX lead and a delivery lead, all introduced before kick-off. We do not staff projects with trainees and we do not swap people out mid-engagement without your agreement.

How do you price projects?

Fixed-price phases for defined scope, or a monthly squad rate for continuous delivery. Discovery is paid and produces a scope, a budget range and a launch date before any build commitment.

Who owns the code and the IP?

You do — source code, infrastructure definitions, documentation and any bespoke models. We sign NDAs and IP assignment up front, and we work inside your repositories and cloud accounts whenever you prefer.

Can you work alongside our in-house team?

Yes. Roughly half of our engagements are blended: your engineers and ours in one backlog, with paired reviews, shared pipelines and a documented handover plan so the knowledge stays with your team.

What happens after launch?

You can take everything in-house, or keep us on a support plan with response and resolution targets, monitoring, security patching and a monthly improvement budget. There is no lock-in either way.
